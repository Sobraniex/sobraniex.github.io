NVIDIA DGX Spark vs. AMD Strix Halo — choose wisely

Two compact local AI machines with 128 GB of unified memory. Which one fits your work?

I have hands-on experience with the DGX Spark, but not with the Strix Halo. This comparison combines that experience with published specifications and independent testing, including StorageReview’s head-to-head review.

The hardware

Both machines offer 128 GB of unified memory and can run large, suitably quantized models locally. NVIDIA lists the DGX Spark at 273 GB/s of memory bandwidth, up to 1 PFLOP of FP4 compute with sparsity, and support for models up to 200 billion parameters. It runs NVIDIA’s Linux-based DGX OS and includes a ConnectX-7 network interface for clustering.

AMD’s Ryzen AI Halo developer platform pairs a 16-core, 32-thread Ryzen AI Max+ 395 with Radeon 8060S graphics, an XDNA 2 NPU, 128 GB of memory, and 256 GB/s of bandwidth. It supports Windows 11 or Linux, so it can also serve as a general-purpose PC.

What the tests show

StorageReview’s vLLM tests found the Spark ahead in most higher-concurrency inference workloads, often by roughly 2–4×. The gap varied sharply by model, quantization, prompt length, and batch size; a few decode-heavy runs were much closer. The Halo held its own in CPU-focused tests and gives you native Windows support. For a single user running a particular model, check a benchmark that matches your actual workload before treating any headline multiplier as a promise.

Why the network port matters

The Spark’s built-in ConnectX-7 makes a two-machine setup practical when a model needs more memory. NVIDIA documents two QSFP ports on each Spark, each capped at 200 Gb/s and configured for Ethernet. The limits of a standalone ConnectX-7 card do not describe the Spark’s ports. A cluster also needs the right cable, software, and model setup; connecting two boxes does not automatically double single-request speed.

Which would I choose?

Choose DGX Spark if your main work is local AI development, CUDA and PyTorch tooling, higher-throughput model serving, or a future two-node cluster. That is the machine I would pick for a dedicated AI workstation.

Choose Strix Halo if you want a flexible Windows or Linux PC that can also run large local models, handle everyday work, and play games. Its x86 platform may be a better fit when one machine has to do several jobs.

Bottom line

For dedicated local AI work, I favor the DGX Spark’s software ecosystem and cluster path. For a more versatile everyday computer that still runs substantial models, the Strix Halo is compelling. Choose for the work you will actually do.

Sources: NVIDIA hardware guide, NVIDIA clustering guide, AMD specifications, and StorageReview tests.
